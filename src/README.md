# src — source code

Every Arduino sketch is in a folder with the same name as its `.ino` file, as the Arduino IDE requires. Open it with **File → Open** and choose the `.ino` file.

The car uses two programmable boards:

| Board | Role |
|---|---|
| **Arduino Nano** (classic ATmega328P since 8 Oct; Nano ESP32 before) | Main controller: TOF distance sensors, BMI160 gyroscope, drive motor (TB6612FNG) and steering servo |
| **ESP32-S3 CAM** (emakefun, OV2640) | Vision: detects the red and green pillars (position in cm), the magenta parking walls and the orange and blue floor lines |

## hardware-tests

One sketch per component, used to validate each part of the car on its own before joining them.

| Sketch | Board | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| [`tof_gyro_sensors_test`](hardware-tests/tof_gyro_sensors_test) | Nano (classic) | All the sensors at once: the 4 TOF400C (VL53L1X, unique I²C address per sensor through XSHUT, Short mode, cm) and the BMI160 gyroscope at 0x69 read through registers (chip ID check, offset calibration, rate and integrated yaw). Also scans the I²C bus. Prints `LEFT:… RIGHT:… BACK:… FRONT:… GZ:… YAW:…` (Serial Plotter friendly). Library: *VL53L1X* by Pololu |
| [`rear_motor_loop_test`](hardware-tests/rear_motor_loop_test) | Nano ESP32 | Drive motor through the TB6612FNG: forward → stop → backward → stop |
| [`servo_simple`](hardware-tests/servo_simple) | Nano ESP32 | Steering servo on D9: left → centre → right sweep, pulse generated by hand (no library) |
| [`servo_calibration`](hardware-tests/servo_calibration) | Nano ESP32 | Interactive: type an angle in the Serial Monitor (or `a` / `d` for ±1°) to find the real steering centre and limits safely |
| [`drive_until_left_open`](hardware-tests/drive_until_left_open) | Nano ESP32 | First driving test: drive straight and brake when the LEFT TOF reads more than 170 cm (the left wall ended) |
| [`servo_only_test`](hardware-tests/servo_only_test) | Nano (classic) | Steering only (pinion + rack): slow sweep centre → left → centre → right to check that the servo never pushes against the ends. Uses `Servo.h` |
| [`wall_follow_nearest_pid`](hardware-tests/wall_follow_nearest_pid) | Nano (classic) | Averages 10 readings of each side TOF, picks the **nearest** wall and keeps that same distance with a PID while driving forward |
| [`wall_keep_both_pid`](hardware-tests/wall_keep_both_pid) | Nano (classic) | Keeps the **left and right** distances measured at the start (does not center): drift = (errorL − errorR) / 2, corrected with a PID |
| [`wall_follow_nearest_gyro`](hardware-tests/wall_follow_nearest_gyro) | Nano (classic) | ✅ **Validated 20 Oct.** Keeps the distance to the **nearest** wall, straight and smooth: distance error → small target heading (max 8°), BMI160 gyroscope → steering. Fixes the zigzag and S-shaped waves of the distance-only PID |
| [`wall_follow_gyro_corner_front`](hardware-tests/wall_follow_gyro_corner_front) | Nano (classic) | 🧪 **In testing (20 Oct).** Same smooth gyro wall following, plus corner handling: when a side TOF reads more than `OPEN_THRESHOLD_MM` (3000 mm) 3 times in a row, `cornerDetected = true` and the turn side is saved; the car then drives straight with the gyroscope and brakes (AIN1 = AIN2 = HIGH) when the **FRONT** TOF reads less than `FRONT_STOP_MM`. Solves the angled side sensors (30°) seeing the opening earlier the farther the car is from the wall |
| [`turn_left_90_pid_brake`](hardware-tests/turn_left_90_pid_brake) | Nano (classic) | ✅ **Validated 21 Oct — 90° turn.** Turns the car 90° to the left with the BMI160 gyroscope: wheels at full left, then a PD on the yaw straightens them little by little as the car approaches 90°, slows down in the last 25°, brakes a few degrees early to absorb inertia (active brake AIN1 = AIN2 = HIGH) and stays still. Gyro at 400 Hz, 1000-sample calibration; tune with `STOP_LEAD_DEG` and `YAW_SCALE` using the printed final difference |
| [`exito_glorioso`](hardware-tests/exito_glorioso) | Nano (classic) | 🏆 **Validated 21 Oct — "Éxito glorioso": first complete straight + corner.** Never hit a wall and always chose the right side in the track tests. Gyro wall following (nearest wall, `MAX_HEADING_DEG = 5`, `MOTOR_SPEED = 50`); when a side TOF reads more than 2400 mm 3 times in a row it remembers the opening and switches the PID to the opposite wall (no brake); the **FRONT** TOF (any reading with a distance, status ignored) below 1400 mm starts the validated 90° gyro turn toward the opening — or, if no opening was seen, toward the farther side — and the car stays braked. Safety: after 40 s of driving it compares both sides and turns anyway. LED D13 on = front TOF not giving a usable reading |
| [`exito_rotundo`](hardware-tests/exito_rotundo) | Nano (classic) | 🏆 **Validated 21 Oct — "Éxito rotundo".** Same gyro wall following, now with an **integral on the heading loop** (`KI_HEADING = 2.5`) so the car no longer drifts to one side, and no front sensor. While driving it keeps the last 5 wall readings of each side; when a side reads more than 2400 mm 3 times in a row it saves the opening and switches the PID to the **opposite wall at the average of its last 5 readings before the opening** (no drift when switching wall). After `RUN_MS` (1.8 s) it brakes: if an opening was seen it turns 90° toward it without comparing; otherwise it averages 10 readings per side and turns toward the farther one. LED D13 on = opening seen |
| [`tof_left_right_print`](hardware-tests/tof_left_right_print) | Nano (classic) | 🔬 **Diagnosis, 24 Oct.** Prints only the LEFT and RIGHT TOF readings **with the status of each reading** (`range valid`, `signal fail`…), ~30 times per second. Showed that every side reading on the black wall was "signal fail" at ~2 m (journal Phase 10) |
| [`tof_roi_budget_test`](hardware-tests/tof_roi_budget_test) | Nano (classic) | 🔬 **Experiment, 24–25 Oct.** Car still in front of a real wall: type the tape-measure distance and it tries every ROI size × timing budget, printing valid / signal-fail / other counts and median, min, max and spread. Found that the sensors at 30° could not see the black wall and that **straight + ROI 16 × 8** measures it within 1–2 cm up to ~45 cm |
| [`camera_webserver_emakefun`](hardware-tests/camera_webserver_emakefun) | ESP32-S3 CAM | Manufacturer's camera web server, used only to check the camera on the bench (needs a 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi network and the other files of the manufacturer's example). **Never used in a round:** Wi-Fi is not allowed during competition rounds (rule 11.10) |

## obstacle-challenge

| File | Board | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| [`pillar_viewer/pillar_viewer.ino`](obstacle-challenge/pillar_viewer) | ESP32-S3 CAM | Pillar detection (HSV colour classification + blob search, closest pillar, correct-side check). Sends every picture and its colour mask over USB |
| [`pillar_viewer/viewer.html`](obstacle-challenge/pillar_viewer/viewer.html) | PC (Chrome / Edge) | **Pillar Vision Lab**: shows what the camera sees, runs the same algorithm in JavaScript and checks it against the board, and lets us tune every parameter live. Open it [online](https://henryalejandrorieraloza-sudo.github.io/WRO-2026-Future-Engineers-Journal/src/obstacle-challenge/pillar_viewer/viewer.html) or as a local file, press *Connect* and choose the camera port (close the Arduino Serial Monitor first) |
| [`obstacle_camera`](obstacle-challenge/obstacle_camera) | ESP32-S3 CAM | ✅ **Current vision code (v3.1, validated 28 Oct).** 320 × 240, camera locked after 5 s, HSV classification, red / magenta majority vote, pillars on 4 × 4 blocks with a solid-shape and plausibility check and a time filter; pillar distance ahead and to the side in **cm** (pinhole model, README §9.3); **orange and blue floor lines** with the long-and-thin test and the **floor-above check** (white floor above the line, so the black wall edge is rejected), blue from hue 110° so the floor is never blue (README §9.6). No false lines on the empty floor or on the wall; orange seen from 16 cm, blue from 9 cm. Sends `$color,x,bottom,correct,blueFound,blueRow,seq,width,distCm,latCm,orangeFound,orangeRow,blueCenterRow,orangeCenterRow` to the Nano at 115200 baud; with `STREAM_TO_PC = true` it also streams pictures to Pillar Vision Lab, with `false` it prints a status every 0.5 s (why each line was accepted or rejected, and a **colour probe**: H, S, V of two fixed boxes, to recalibrate under any light) |
| [`straight_pillars`](obstacle-challenge/straight_pillars) | Nano (classic) | ✅ **Validated 27 Oct — one straight with pillars, every case passes.** Odometry map of the straight (encoder 15.1 ticks / cm + gyro), every pillar put on the map, lateral goal 17 cm from the pillar on its correct side → heading `atan2(error, distance)` (max 45°) → gyro PD → servo. Aims at the next pillar as soon as the camera passes the current one; clears it 5 cm after its centre. Waits for START (D10), stops after `RUN_CM` or if the camera stops talking (README §9.4–9.5) |
| [`line_test`](obstacle-challenge/line_test) | Nano (classic) | 🔬 **Measurement, 27 Oct.** Motor off: prints 5 times per second whether the camera sees the orange and blue lines, their lowest row and their row straight ahead, how many messages had them, and the pillar. Used to measure the row → cm table of README §9.6 |

## Arduino IDE settings

**Nano ESP32:** *Tools → Board → Arduino ESP32 Boards → Arduino Nano ESP32*.

**Arduino Nano (classic, ATmega328P)** — used since 8 October, when the Nano ESP32 was damaged: *Tools → Board → Arduino AVR Boards → Arduino Nano*. If the upload fails with `not in sync` or `resp=0x00`, set *Tools → Processor → ATmega328P (Old Bootloader)*. The steering in these sketches uses `Servo.h` (500–2500 µs) and the TOF sensors the *VL53L1X* library by Pololu.

**ESP32-S3 CAM:**

| Option | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Board | ESP32S3 Dev Module |
| USB CDC On Boot | Enabled |
| Flash Size | 8MB (64Mb) |
| Partition Scheme | 8M with spiffs (3MB APP/1.5MB SPIFFS) |
| PSRAM | OPI PSRAM |

If the camera upload stays at `Connecting...`: hold **BOOT**, press and release **RESET**, then release **BOOT**.
If the Nano ESP32 upload fails with `dfu-util` errors: double-press **RESET** (the LED "breathes" green), select the port again and upload.
