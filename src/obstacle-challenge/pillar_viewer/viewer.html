Pillar Vision Lab

Live picture from the ESP32S3-CAM with the same steps as closest_pillar_test: colors, blobs, the split of touching pillars and the closest-pillar decision. The page re-runs the Arduino algorithm on every picture and checks it gives the same answer as the camera. It also finds the orange and blue floor lines and the magenta parking walls (page only for now) and works with any picture size (160×120, 320×240…).

Pillars are only searched in the band between the yellow line (ignore top) and the red NEAR line (counted in rows up from the bottom edge); drag either line on the picture. Side zone: a pillar whose centre is within ± this many pixels of the middle is CENTER. A red pillar is on the correct side once it is left of the zone, a green one once it is right of it: green halo = correct side, red halo = not yet. Lines and magenta are searched from the dotted purple line down to the bottom edge; a line whose lowest row passes the cyan trigger line counts as "reached" (time to turn and count a corner).

Calibrate colors

Press a color, then drag a box over that object on the picture (only over the object). The page reads the real camera pixels inside the box and sets that color's thresholds by itself. Floor and wall are only checked, so you can see if they would be mistaken for a color. Do every color you can, in the light you will race in; each new box for the same color adds to it.

The algorithm, step by step

1Picture

160×120 RGB565 from the camera, read with readPixel() (mirror / flip applied).

2Colors

Every pixel to HSV and classify(): red, green or nothing. Orange, blue and magenta are drawn on top in their own colors.

3Blobs

BFS joins touching pixels of one color. Under MIN_PIXELS is noise (grey dashes).

4Closest

frontPillarOfBlob() keeps the front part, isCloser() picks the lowest bottom.

Thresholds

Every parameter of the Arduino code. They start with the robot's values; move them to try new values live (changed ones turn orange). The page saves them automatically in this browser (separately for 160×120 and 320×240), so they are still there after reloading or reconnecting. The robot keeps its own values until you press Copy as Arduino code and paste the lines into the sketch.
